The Bank of Japan is expected to leave interest rates unchanged at its October meeting, but the decision could still mark an important shift in its policy thinking, according to a preview from Daiwa Securities. The broker expects the board to conclude that underlying inflation has reached 2%, a step that would move the central bank from a phase of lifting inflation toward its target to one focused on keeping it stable around that level.

Daiwa cited media reports that economic conditions and prices have continued to track the BOJ’s outlook since September, and that policymakers do not see upside inflation risks as strong enough to justify back-to-back rate increases. Surveys published in October also showed inflation expectations among households and businesses easing. Even so, concern about an inflation overshoot remains high. Cost pass-through is broadening at the consumer level, and rising crude oil prices have revived upstream price pressures, with some within the BOJ expecting pass-through to accelerate into next year.

Governor Kazuo Ueda signalled the change in tone earlier this month, saying that with underlying inflation now close to 2%, the bank can no longer tolerate further increases and is aiming to stabilise it. Daiwa expects the Outlook Report to replace its description of inflation as approaching 2% with wording that it is broadly consistent with the target, and argues that if the report continues to flag upside inflation risks, that would provide grounds for considering an early hike.

The broker expects board member Hajime Takata to dissent and propose a hike, as he did in July, citing strong global demand, including for AI-related products. Naoki Tamura, who has spoken of raising rates every few months toward a neutral level of around 2%, is seen supporting a hold so soon after September’s increase.

On forecasts, Daiwa expects the board to lift its FY26 growth projection to 0.8% from 0.6% and FY27 core inflation to 2.5% from 2.4% on the back of higher oil prices, while trimming FY26 core-core inflation slightly to 2.4%. The report is unlikely to factor in a planned cut to the consumption tax on food, which Daiwa estimates could lower core inflation by roughly 1.5 percentage points in FY27.

Ueda’s post-meeting press conference will be pivotal. Daiwa will watch whether he keeps describing rate decisions as being made at each meeting or shifts toward language pointing to the next meeting, which markets could read as a stronger signal of a December hike. With the policy rate already above the lower end of the BOJ’s estimated neutral range, his assessment of financial conditions will also shape expectations for how quickly further tightening follows.

Reserve Bank of Australia Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter said the central bank has not yet finished its fight against inflation, reinforcing expectations that policymakers will raise interest rates again when they meet on November 3.

Speaking in an interview with Nine Network’s 60 Minutes, broadcast on Sunday evening Australian time, Hunter noted that the board had delivered four rate increases this year to tackle persistent price pressures. Asked whether that tightening had been enough to tame inflation, she replied “not quite yet.” She declined to front-run the board’s next decision, but stressed that policymakers remained committed to bringing inflation down.

Her remarks build on comments from Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser on Wednesday, when he said the inflation outlook had become materially worse since the bank’s August assessment. Those remarks had already lifted market expectations for a hike at the November meeting.

Hunter, who oversees economic forecasting at the RBA, pointed to the ongoing US-Iran war as a source of renewed pressure. Higher oil prices linked to the conflict are beginning to become embedded in some supply chains, she said, adding that the bank expects more of that pass-through to come. The comment suggests the RBA is increasingly concerned that the energy shock is spreading beyond fuel prices into the broader cost base of the economy, which would make inflation more persistent and harder to bring back down.

The RBA lifted its cash rate in late September to 4.6%, the highest level in 15 years. A further increase would add downward pressure on Australian property prices, which have already been falling. Hunter said the decline in house prices was not, so far, creating systemic financial stress across the system, a signal that the bank does not see housing weakness as a reason to hold off.

Attention now turns to Wednesday, when Australia’s third-quarter consumer price data are released. The trimmed mean measure of underlying inflation is expected at 3.6%, broadly in line with the previous quarter. Governor Michele Bullock and other policymakers are also scheduled to appear before parliament the same day, giving them another opportunity to set out their thinking ahead of the decision. A reading in line with forecasts, combined with the oil-driven pressure Hunter described, would likely leave a November hike as the market’s base case.

[2026-10-26 00:22 UTC] PBOC is expected to set the USD/CNY reference rate at 6.6691 – Reuters estimate
URL: https://investinglive.com/central-banks/pboc-is-expected-to-set-the-usd-cny-reference-rate-at-6-6691-reuters-estimate/
Tags: CNY, CNH, pboc
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summary

PBOC CNY reference rate setting for the trading session ahead.

The People’s Bank of China is due to set the daily USD/CNY reference rate at around 0115 GMT (2115 US Eastern time), a fixing that remains one of the most closely watched signals in Asian foreign exchange markets.

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system, under which the renminbi (yuan) is allowed to trade within a prescribed band around a central reference rate, or midpoint, set each trading day by the PBOC. The current trading band permits the currency to move plus or minus 2% from the official midpoint during onshore trading hours.

Each morning, the PBOC determines the midpoint based on a range of inputs. These include the previous day’s closing price, movements in major currencies, particularly the US dollar, broader international FX conditions, and domestic economic considerations such as capital flows, growth momentum and financial stability objectives. The midpoint is not a purely mechanical calculation, allowing policymakers discretion to guide market expectations.

Once the midpoint is announced, onshore USD/CNY is free to trade within the allowable band. If market pressures push the yuan toward either edge of that range, the central bank may step in to smooth volatility. Intervention can take the form of direct buying or selling of yuan, adjustments to liquidity conditions, or guidance through state-owned banks.

As a result, the daily fixing is often interpreted as a policy signal rather than just a technical reference point. A stronger-than-expected CNY midpoint is typically read as a sign the PBOC is leaning against depreciation pressure, while a weaker fixing for the CNY can indicate tolerance for a softer currency, often in response to dollar strength or domestic economic headwinds.

In periods of heightened global volatility, such as shifts in US rate expectations, trade tensions or capital flow pressures, the fixing takes on added significance. For investors, it provides insight into Beijing’s currency priorities, balancing competitiveness, capital stability and financial market confidence.
