[2026-10-25 22:18 UTC] BOJ preview: Daiwa sees hold, but 2% inflation call could open door to December hike
URL: https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-preview-daiwa-sees-hold-but-2-inflation-call-could-open-door-to-december-hike/
Tags: BOJ, Bonds, JPY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summary

Daiwa expects the BOJ to hold rates this month but declare underlying inflation has reached 2%, shifting focus to stabilising it there. Board member Takata may again propose a hike, and Ueda’s wording will be closely watched for December hints.

A hold is largely the base case, so the yen and JGB yields are likely to take their cue from the language rather than the decision. A formal declaration that underlying inflation has reached 2%, a Takata dissent and any shift by Ueda toward “next meeting” phrasing would together read as hawkish, supporting the yen and pushing short-end yields higher as markets price a December move. Higher crude prices are a key upside risk, with oil already reigniting upstream cost pressure and feeding the BOJ’s concern about inflation overshooting. A guarded Ueda who stresses a broad review of financial conditions could see December pricing trimmed and the yen soften.

---The BOJ is expected to stand pat on rates while quietly moving the goalposts, from getting inflation up to 2% to stopping it from running beyond it, which keeps December firmly in play.

Daiwa expects the BOJ to keep rates unchanged at its October meeting, with October surveys showing softer household and business inflation expectations

The board is likely to conclude that underlying inflation has reached 2%, shifting policy from lifting inflation toward stabilising it around that level

Board member Hajime Takata may again propose a rate hike, while Naoki Tamura is seen backing a hold after September’s increase

Daiwa expects the Outlook Report to raise the FY26 growth forecast to 0.8% from 0.6% and FY27 core CPI to 2.5% from 2.4%, and to trim FY26 core-core CPI to 2.4%

Higher crude prices have reignited upstream inflation pressure, and the BOJ’s policy rate already sits above the lower end of its estimated neutral range of around 1% to 2.5%

Ueda’s press conference will be watched for December hints, particularly any shift from “every meeting” language toward the next meeting

The Bank of Japan is expected to leave interest rates unchanged at its October meeting, but the decision could still mark an important shift in its policy thinking, according to a preview from Daiwa Securities. The broker expects the board to conclude that underlying inflation has reached 2%, a step that would move the central bank from a phase of lifting inflation toward its target to one focused on keeping it stable around that level.

Daiwa cited media reports that economic conditions and prices have continued to track the BOJ’s outlook since September, and that policymakers do not see upside inflation risks as strong enough to justify back-to-back rate increases. Surveys published in October also showed inflation expectations among households and businesses easing. Even so, concern about an inflation overshoot remains high. Cost pass-through is broadening at the consumer level, and rising crude oil prices have revived upstream price pressures, with some within the BOJ expecting pass-through to accelerate into next year.

Governor Kazuo Ueda signalled the change in tone earlier this month, saying that with underlying inflation now close to 2%, the bank can no longer tolerate further increases and is aiming to stabilise it. Daiwa expects the Outlook Report to replace its description of inflation as approaching 2% with wording that it is broadly consistent with the target, and argues that if the report continues to flag upside inflation risks, that would provide grounds for considering an early hike.

The broker expects board member Hajime Takata to dissent and propose a hike, as he did in July, citing strong global demand, including for AI-related products. Naoki Tamura, who has spoken of raising rates every few months toward a neutral level of around 2%, is seen supporting a hold so soon after September’s increase.

On forecasts, Daiwa expects the board to lift its FY26 growth projection to 0.8% from 0.6% and FY27 core inflation to 2.5% from 2.4% on the back of higher oil prices, while trimming FY26 core-core inflation slightly to 2.4%. The report is unlikely to factor in a planned cut to the consumption tax on food, which Daiwa estimates could lower core inflation by roughly 1.5 percentage points in FY27.

Ueda’s post-meeting press conference will be pivotal. Daiwa will watch whether he keeps describing rate decisions as being made at each meeting or shifts toward language pointing to the next meeting, which markets could read as a stronger signal of a December hike. With the policy rate already above the lower end of the BOJ’s estimated neutral range, his assessment of financial conditions will also shape expectations for how quickly further tightening follows.

[2026-10-25 21:22 UTC] Preview: RBNZ set to lift OCR to 3% ahead of election as oil and weak kiwi fuel inflation
URL: https://investinglive.com/central-banks/preview-rbnz-set-to-lift-ocr-to-3-ahead-of-election-as-oil-and-weak-kiwi-fuel-inflation/
Tags: RBNZ, NZD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summary

The RBNZ is expected to raise the OCR 25bp to 3% on Wednesday, 10 days before NZ’s election, as oil and a weak kiwi stoke inflation risks. Markets price about a 90% chance, and Westpac sees the OCR possibly reaching 4% by September 2027.

With a hike close to fully priced, the New Zealand dollar’s reaction is likely to hinge on guidance rather than the move itself, and the risk is asymmetric: a hold would catch markets badly offside and could trigger a sharp kiwi selloff. A hike paired with a clear tightening bias would be the most supportive outcome for the NZD and would push front-end yields higher, while “wait and see” language could see some of the priced peak trimmed. Oil remains the key external driver: further gains in crude linked to the Middle East conflict would feed straight into New Zealand’s fuel-heavy inflation outlook and strengthen the case for more hikes into 2027. AUD/NZD traders also face back-to-back central bank risk, with theRBA expected to hike again on November 3.

A pause looked likely in September, but rising fuel costs and a sliding kiwi have turned Wednesday’s RBNZ meeting into a near-certain pre-election hike.

Economists and markets expect the RBNZ to raise the OCR by 25bp to 3% on Wednesday, its third straight hike after moves in July and September

Expectations for a pause after September have reversed as oil prices surged, the New Zealand dollar weakened and inflation pressures proved sticky

Annual CPI eased to 3.7% in the September quarter, below the RBNZ’s 3.9% forecast, but some economists see the December quarter above 4.5%

Markets price roughly a 90% chance of a hike and a peak near 3.5% by February; Westpac sees the OCR possibly reaching 4% by September 2027

ASB warns that a hold would likely sink the kiwi, with guidance on further tightening seen as the key market driver

The decision is due at 01:00 GMT Wednesday (21:00 ET Tuesday), ahead of New Zealand’s November 7 general election

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand is widely expected to raise its Official Cash Rate by 25 basis points to 3% on Wednesday, delivering a third consecutive hike as rising fuel costs and a weak currency threaten to push inflation further above its target band.

The call marks a turnaround from just weeks ago. After the RBNZ lifted the OCR to 2.75% in early September, Governor Anna Breman hinted at a pause, and bank economists were divided on whether October would bring another move. That debate has largely closed. According to interest.co.nz, economists at the country’s major banks now see a hike as almost certain, pointing to the surge in oil prices, further weakness in the New Zealand dollar and persistently sticky price pressures.

A Bloomberg survey also found that a majority of economists expect the OCR to rise to 3% this week. Inflation slowed in the third quarter, with annual CPI easing to 3.7% from 4.1% in June and coming in below the RBNZ’s 3.9% forecast. Many analysts expect it to accelerate again in the fourth quarter, however, with some projecting a December quarter reading of around 4.7%, which would leave inflation outside the 1% to 3% target band for longer than the central bank had projected.

Markets are positioned accordingly. ASB economists said pricing implies roughly a 90% probability of a 25 basis point increase, with a peak cash rate of around 3.5% expected by February. Westpac sees the tightening cycle running further, with the OCR possibly reaching 4% by September 2027. ASB has also warned that the prospect of hikes has been supporting the New Zealand dollar, and that a decision to hold would likely send the currency lower.

The timing adds a political dimension. The meeting falls just 10 days before the November 7 general election, although economists broadly expect the committee to set policy on economic grounds alone. Some argue the bank should go beyond a single move and signal that more tightening is coming, while others expect a short statement that leaves options open.

The decision is due at 01:00 GMT on Wednesday (21:00 ET on Tuesday). With a hike largely priced, the tone of the accompanying statement is likely to determine the market reaction.

[2026-10-25 20:28 UTC] RBA’s Hunter says inflation job not done after four hikes, November rise in view
URL: https://investinglive.com/central-banks/rba-s-hunter-says-inflation-job-not-done-after-four-hikes-november-rise-in-view/
Tags: RBA, AUD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summary

RBA Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter said Sunday the bank has not yet done enough to tame inflation after four hikes this year, adding to bets on another rise on November 3 as oil costs from the US-Iran war seep into supply chains. CPI is due Wednesday.

The comments should keep the Australian dollar supported, at the margin, on rate differentials and hold short-dated Australian government bond yields elevated, with markets likely to lean further toward a November hike unless Wednesday’s inflation data surprise lower. Oil is the swing variable: renewed escalation in the US-Iran war that lifts crude would deepen the RBA’s concern about second-round effects and raise the risk of tightening beyond November. A sustained easing in crude, by contrast, would give the board more room to signal a pause after its next move. Property-linked stocks and consumer discretionary names on the ASX remain exposed to a higher-for-longer policy path.

Four hikes in, the RBA’s forecasting chief says the job is not finished, with war-driven oil costs working their way into supply chains and a fifth increase looking likely on November 3.

RBA Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter said the bank has “not quite yet” done enough to bring inflation under control, in a television interview aired Sunday evening Australian time

She declined to pre-empt the board’s decision but said policymakers remain committed to getting inflation down; the next meeting is November 3

Hunter said higher oil prices linked to the US-Iran war are becoming embedded in some supply chains, with more of that effect expected

Deputy Governor AndrewHauser said on Wednesdaythe inflation outlook is materially worse than the bank assessed in August

The cash rate stands at 4.6%, a 15-year high, after four hikes this year; Hunter said falling house prices are not causing systemic financial stress

Third-quarter CPI is due Wednesday, with trimmed mean inflation expected at 3.6%, similar to the prior quarter

Reserve Bank of Australia Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter said the central bank has not yet finished its fight against inflation, reinforcing expectations that policymakers will raise interest rates again when they meet on November 3.

Speaking in an interview with Nine Network’s 60 Minutes, broadcast on Sunday evening Australian time, Hunter noted that the board had delivered four rate increases this year to tackle persistent price pressures. Asked whether that tightening had been enough to tame inflation, she replied “not quite yet.” She declined to front-run the board’s next decision, but stressed that policymakers remained committed to bringing inflation down.

Her remarks build on comments from Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser on Wednesday, when he said the inflation outlook had become materially worse since the bank’s August assessment. Those remarks had already lifted market expectations for a hike at the November meeting.

Hunter, who oversees economic forecasting at the RBA, pointed to the ongoing US-Iran war as a source of renewed pressure. Higher oil prices linked to the conflict are beginning to become embedded in some supply chains, she said, adding that the bank expects more of that pass-through to come. The comment suggests the RBA is increasingly concerned that the energy shock is spreading beyond fuel prices into the broader cost base of the economy, which would make inflation more persistent and harder to bring back down.

The RBA lifted its cash rate in late September to 4.6%, the highest level in 15 years. A further increase would add downward pressure on Australian property prices, which have already been falling. Hunter said the decline in house prices was not, so far, creating systemic financial stress across the system, a signal that the bank does not see housing weakness as a reason to hold off.

Attention now turns to Wednesday, when Australia’s third-quarter consumer price data are released. The trimmed mean measure of underlying inflation is expected at 3.6%, broadly in line with the previous quarter. Governor Michele Bullock and other policymakers are also scheduled to appear before parliament the same day, giving them another opportunity to set out their thinking ahead of the decision. A reading in line with forecasts, combined with the oil-driven pressure Hunter described, would likely leave a November hike as the market’s base case.
