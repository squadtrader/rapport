It is an immense honor to speak today in celebration of Michel Camdessus. Few leaders have shaped modern international economic cooperation with such profound moral clarity, intellectual depth, and enduring goodwill as Mr. Camdessus has. He taught a generation of policymakers that macroeconomic policy is never merely technical; at its best, it is an act of shared global stewardship. That wisdom was made especially clear in the mid-1980s, when global imbalances placed Japan's current account surplus at the heart of international economic debate. Mr. Camdessus was among the most articulate voices calling for surplus nations to act as locomotives for global demand. Japan embraced that call, yet history took an unexpected turn: the post-Plaza Accord adjustments narrowed the surplus only briefly before it transformed into a structural, income-driven surplus that persists to this day. In honoring Mr. Camdessus, the best tribute we can offer is to examine these difficult economic realities with the same analytical honesty he brought to global governance.
