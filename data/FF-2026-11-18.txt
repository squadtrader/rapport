Members began their discussion of financial conditions by noting that market pricing still implied an expectation that central banks in most countries would raise their policy rate a few times over the year ahead, particularly in economies where monetary policy was currently judged to be accommodative. Even so, expectations for policy rates had eased somewhat since the previous meeting, including in response to a reassessment of the future profitability of some artificial intelligence (AI)-related sectors. Australian policy rate expectations had largely followed these global trends. Government bond yields had eased in tandem with policy rate expectations over October. Nonetheless, long-term government bond yields remained higher than in early August across most advanced economies, including Australia. Members noted that the increases since August had not been driven by a rise in long-term inflation expectations – financial market measures of longer-term inflation compensation remained broadly consistent with central bank targets, though shorter-term measures were higher owing to the elevated near-term outlook for inflation in many countries.
