Summary

PBOC CNY reference rate setting for the trading session ahead.

The People’s Bank of China is due to set the daily USD/CNY reference rate at around 0115 GMT (2115 US Eastern time), a fixing that remains one of the most closely watched signals in Asian foreign exchange markets.

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system, under which the renminbi (yuan) is allowed to trade within a prescribed band around a central reference rate, or midpoint, set each trading day by the PBOC. The current trading band permits the currency to move plus or minus 2% from the official midpoint during onshore trading hours.

Each morning, the PBOC determines the midpoint based on a range of inputs. These include the previous day’s closing price, movements in major currencies, particularly the US dollar, broader international FX conditions, and domestic economic considerations such as capital flows, growth momentum and financial stability objectives. The midpoint is not a purely mechanical calculation, allowing policymakers discretion to guide market expectations.

Once the midpoint is announced, onshore USD/CNY is free to trade within the allowable band. If market pressures push the yuan toward either edge of that range, the central bank may step in to smooth volatility. Intervention can take the form of direct buying or selling of yuan, adjustments to liquidity conditions, or guidance through state-owned banks.

As a result, the daily fixing is often interpreted as a policy signal rather than just a technical reference point. A stronger-than-expected CNY midpoint is typically read as a sign the PBOC is leaning against depreciation pressure, while a weaker fixing for the CNY can indicate tolerance for a softer currency, often in response to dollar strength or domestic economic headwinds.

In periods of heightened global volatility, such as shifts in US rate expectations, trade tensions or capital flow pressures, the fixing takes on added significance. For investors, it provides insight into Beijing’s currency priorities, balancing competitiveness, capital stability and financial market confidence.

The Bank of Japan said underlying inflation is now generally consistent with its 2% price stability target, a long-awaited declaration that strengthens the case for further rate hikes. It kept its policy rate unchanged at 1.25% on Friday.

In its quarterly Outlook Report, the central bank said underlying consumer price inflation is at a level broadly in line with the target and is expected to stay around there. It pointed to a persistent sense of labour shortage, an interaction between rising wages and prices, and medium- to long-term inflation expectations of about 2%. The bank described the risks to prices as skewed to the upside. It warned that inflation expectations could rise and push underlying inflation above target as firms increasingly lift wages and prices.

The board’s median forecast for core inflation, which excludes fresh food, was raised to 2.6% for fiscal 2027 from 2.4% previously, reflecting the rise in crude oil prices since the summer. The fiscal 2026 forecast was unchanged at 2.5%, and fiscal 2028 stayed at 2.0%. The bank expects core inflation to rise to a level clearly above 2% in the first half of the projection period, driven by oil, higher prices for semiconductors and other items, and a weaker yen. It then sees inflation easing toward around 2% as the effect of high oil prices fades. Its projections assume Dubai crude falls from around $105 a barrel to around $70 by the end of the forecast period, and they exclude the effect of a proposed cut to the consumption tax on food.

Growth forecasts were little changed, with real GDP seen expanding 0.7% in fiscal 2026 and 2027 and 0.8% in fiscal 2028. The bank expects the economy to keep growing at a slower pace this fiscal year, as higher energy costs linked to the Middle East weigh on activity, supported by government measures and AI-related demand.

The policy board voted 8-1 to hold rates. Board member Hajime Takata dissented, proposing a hike to 1.5% on the grounds that with underlying inflation at target, the bank should speed up moves toward the neutral rate. No board member dissented in favour of a cut, removing a risk flagged ahead of the meeting.

The bank also unanimously agreed to more than double the pace of its ETF sales from January 2027, to about 860 billion yen a year on a book value basis from about 330 billion yen. That would shorten the time needed to sell its holdings to about 43 years from around 112.

The Bank of Japan kept its short-term policy rate unchanged at 1.25% on Friday, as widely expected. The decision came with a hawkish dissent and a sharp increase in the pace at which the central bank will sell its holdings of exchange-traded funds.

The policy board voted 8-1 to keep guiding the uncollateralised overnight call rate at around 1.25%. Board member Hajime Takata voted against and proposed raising the rate to around 1.5%. He argued that with underlying consumer price inflation having reached the bank’s 2% price stability target, the situation had moved into a new phase. In his view, the bank needs to speed up the pace of bringing its policy rate closer to the neutral rate, so that it can respond nimbly. His proposal was defeated by a majority vote.

The vote removes one of the main risks flagged ahead of the meeting. Two board members who opposed September’s rate hike had been seen as possible dissenters in favour of a cut. Analysts had warned that such a move could undercut a hawkish message and weaken the yen. Instead, the only dissent came from the hawkish side.

The board also voted unanimously to more than double the pace of its ETF sales. The bank began selling the funds in January this year at about 330 billion yen a year on a book value basis, equivalent to around 0.05% of market trading value. From January 2027, it will sell at about 0.1% of trading value, or roughly 860 billion yen a year on a book value basis. On a market value basis, that equates to about 2.5 trillion yen a year. The bank said the sales had proceeded smoothly without a major market impact, while trading in the stock market had expanded. It retained the option to adjust or suspend sales temporarily in response to market conditions. It left its sales of Japanese real estate investment trusts unchanged at about 5 billion yen a year.

Markets were pricing about an 80% chance of a hike in December going into the meeting. Attention now turns to the quarterly Outlook Report, which analysts expect to effectively declare that underlying inflation has reached 2%. Governor Kazuo Ueda’s press conference later in the afternoon will be watched for any signal on the timing of the next move.
