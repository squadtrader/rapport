[2026-11-11 00:09 UTC] NIESR sees UK inflation above BoE target for four years, peaking at 4.4% in 2027

Tags: BOE, GBP

Summary

NIESR forecasts UK inflation will peak at 4.4% in February 2027 and not return to the BoE’s 2% target until late 2030, as Iran war energy costs feed into wages. It sees just one more BoE hike, in February, versus two or three priced by markets.

A longer inflation overshoot driven by the Iran war’s energy shock keeps oil prices at the centre of the UK rate outlook: every sustained rise in crude raises the risk of further second-round effects on wages. NIESR’s call for only one more hike contrasts with market pricing of two or three. Its view that rates may need to reach around 5% points the other way, and supports the case for gilt yields and sterling to stay sensitive to energy-led data surprises. The critique of the October budget adds fiscal credibility risk on top, which could weigh on longer-dated gilts if borrowing plans keep slipping.

---NIESR sees a longer inflation overshoot in Britain, and a BoE that may be doing less than the job requires.

NIESR forecasts UK inflation will peak at 4.4% in February 2027, up from a 3.8% peak in its July forecast

It sees inflation above the BoE’s 2% target in 2027, 2028 and 2029, returning to target in late 2030

It expects one more BoE hike, in February, while markets price two or three by end-2027, and says rates may need to reach around 5%

The 2026 growth forecast is raised to 1.6% from 1.1%, slowing to 1.1% in 2027 and 0.9% in 2028

Unemployment is seen rising to a peak of 5.1% in 2031, with wage growth averaging 3.9% over three years

NIESR said the October budget did little to strengthen the public finances

UK inflation will stay above the Bank of England’s 2% target for the next four years as the Iran war’s energy shock feeds through into wages, the National Institute of Economic and Social Research (NIESR) forecast on Tuesday. The think-tank also raised its outlook for growth.

It now expects inflation to peak at 4.4% in February 2027, up from the 3.8% peak it forecast in July. It sees inflation remaining above target through 2027, 2028 and 2029 before returning to 2% in late 2030, a year later than it previously expected. Second-round effects on wages are the main reason for the delay.

The outlook for policy is where NIESR parts company with markets. Investors are pricing two or three quarter-point rate hikes by the end of next year, but the think-tank expects the BoE to raise rates only once more, in February. NIESR also said rates would need to rise to around 5% to bring inflation back to target over the medium term, which suggests it thinks a single hike would not be enough to meet the goal on time. The BoE raised Bank Rate to 4% from 3.75% last week, its first increase in more than three years.

NIESR Director David Aikman said the economy had held up better than expected this year but now faces the twin pressures of higher energy prices and rising global borrowing costs. He said the policy response on both the monetary and fiscal sides is a domestic choice, and that decisions on both fronts are being put off.

On the fiscal side, NIESR said finance minister John Healey’s October budget did little to strengthen the public finances. Difficult decisions on tax and spending had largely been deferred, it said, and plans to reduce borrowing lacked credibility.

Growth has been more resilient than expected. NIESR raised its 2026 forecast to 1.6% from 1.1%, citing stronger government spending and business investment, partly driven by AI. It expects growth to slow to 1.1% in 2027 and 0.9% in 2028. Unemployment is projected to edge up from 4.8% in 2026 to a peak of 5.1% in 2031, with wage growth averaging 3.9% over the next three years.

The gap between NIESR’s single-hike forecast and market pricing will be tested at the BoE’s next meetings, with energy prices and wage settlements the key data to watch.

NIESR is Britain’s longest-established independent economic research institute. It was founded in 1938 and is based in London. It is a non-profit charity, independent of government and political parties, and it publishes regular forecasts for the UK and global economies, which are widely watched alongside those from the Bank of England and the government’s fiscal watchdog. Its director, David Aikman, joined in 2023 from the Bank of England, where he worked on financial stability.

[2026-11-11 00:22 UTC] PBOC is expected to set the USD/CNY reference rate at 6.6855 – Reuters estimate

Tags: CNY, CNH, pboc
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summary

PBOC CNY reference rate setting for the trading session ahead.

The People’s Bank of China is due to set the daily USD/CNY reference rate at around 0115 GMT (2115 US Eastern time), a fixing that remains one of the most closely watched signals in Asian foreign exchange markets.

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system, under which the renminbi (yuan) is allowed to trade within a prescribed band around a central reference rate, or midpoint, set each trading day by the PBOC. The current trading band permits the currency to move plus or minus 2% from the official midpoint during onshore trading hours.

Each morning, the PBOC determines the midpoint based on a range of inputs. These include the previous day’s closing price, movements in major currencies, particularly the US dollar, broader international FX conditions, and domestic economic considerations such as capital flows, growth momentum and financial stability objectives. The midpoint is not a purely mechanical calculation, allowing policymakers discretion to guide market expectations.

Once the midpoint is announced, onshore USD/CNY is free to trade within the allowable band. If market pressures push the yuan toward either edge of that range, the central bank may step in to smooth volatility. Intervention can take the form of direct buying or selling of yuan, adjustments to liquidity conditions, or guidance through state-owned banks.

As a result, the daily fixing is often interpreted as a policy signal rather than just a technical reference point. A stronger-than-expected CNY midpoint is typically read as a sign the PBOC is leaning against depreciation pressure, while a weaker fixing for the CNY can indicate tolerance for a softer currency, often in response to dollar strength or domestic economic headwinds.

In periods of heightened global volatility, such as shifts in US rate expectations, trade tensions or capital flow pressures, the fixing takes on added significance. For investors, it provides insight into Beijing’s currency priorities, balancing competitiveness, capital stability and financial market confidence.

[2026-11-11 04:14 UTC] Three reasons Goldman has added another Fed rate hike to its forecast, higher and higher!
URL: https://investinglive.com/central-banks/three-reasons-goldman-has-added-another-fed-rate-hike-to-its-forecast-higher-and-higher/
Tags: Fed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summary

Goldman Sachs now sees 25bp Fed hikes in December 2026 and March 2027, adding a third hike to the cycle after a third straight month of hot inflation, and has pushed its first rate cut to December 2027. January is possible for the March move.

A tier-one bank moving to three hikes in the cycle supports the US dollar and front-end Treasury yields, and adds to pressure on rate-sensitive equities and gold. Pushing the first cut back to December 2027 extends the period of restrictive policy, which could weigh on long-duration assets. The reversal of Goldman’s late-October call also shows how much oil is driving the outlook: the Middle East escalation it flagged as a risk has fed directly into the inflation data. Further gains in crude, including from attacks on Hormuz shipping and Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline, would raise the odds of the hike coming in January rather than March.

Three hot inflation prints have turned Goldman’s “maybe” on a March hike back into a “likely”.

Goldman Sachs still expects a 25bp Fed hike in December and has added another 25bp hike in March 2027

The March move could come in January, but March is slightly more likely given expected milder November and December inflation data

Goldman pushed its forecast for the first rate cut back a quarter, to December 2027

It expects most FOMC members to signal a third hike in the December dot plot

The bank still sees inflation meaningfully lower by end-2027 as tariff, energy and AI pressures fade

Goldman Sachs now expects the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates again in March 2027, following a quarter-point hike in December, after a third consecutive month of hotter-than-expected inflation data, the bank’s chief US economist David Mericle said in a note on Tuesday.

Goldman continues to forecast a 25 basis point increase at the Fed’s December 8-9 meeting, which would be the second hike of the current tightening cycle. The new March call adds a third. Mericle said the move could be brought forward to January, but March is slightly more likely. Inflation readings for November and December are expected to be relatively mild, while larger price increases early in the year are likely to lift January’s figures. The bank also pushed back its forecast for the first rate cut by a quarter, to December 2027.

The change follows October’s consumer price report, which showed a larger-than-expected rise. Goldman expects the run of firm inflation data to lead most Fed policymakers to signal a third hike in the December dot plot, and said a strong January inflation report would make delivering that hike the path of least resistance. The bank remains confident, however, that inflation will be meaningfully lower by the end of 2027 as the effects of tariffs, the energy shock and AI-related price pressures fade.

The forecast reverses a call Goldman made only weeks ago. In late October, the bank had removed a March hike from its base case. It warned at the time that an escalation in the Middle East that drove oil prices higher, combined with persistent inflation, could change the Fed’s path. Its asset management arm has also cautioned that hotter inflation could force a more aggressive tightening cycle extending into 2027.

Goldman’s revised outlook is broadly in line with Fed commentary this week. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said further gradual hikes seem appropriate, Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called the CPI report borderline disturbing, and Governor Christopher Waller had said a hot reading could lead him to back a larger move. Markets are pricing a quarter-point December hike as close to certain, with a small chance of a half-point move.

With the federal funds target range currently at 3.75% to 4%, December’s decision and its updated projections will show whether policymakers are ready to signal the further tightening Goldman expects.
