NIESR forecasts UK inflation will peak at 4.4% in February 2027 and not return to the BoE’s 2% target until late 2030, as Iran war energy costs feed into wages. It sees just one more BoE hike, in February, versus two or three priced by markets.

UK inflation will stay above the Bank of England’s 2% target for the next four years as the Iran war’s energy shock feeds through into wages, the National Institute of Economic and Social Research (NIESR) forecast on Tuesday. The think-tank also raised its outlook for growth.

It now expects inflation to peak at 4.4% in February 2027, up from the 3.8% peak it forecast in July. It sees inflation remaining above target through 2027, 2028 and 2029 before returning to 2% in late 2030, a year later than it previously expected. Second-round effects on wages are the main reason for the delay.

The outlook for policy is where NIESR parts company with markets. Investors are pricing two or three quarter-point rate hikes by the end of next year, but the think-tank expects the BoE to raise rates only once more, in February. NIESR also said rates would need to rise to around 5% to bring inflation back to target over the medium term, which suggests it thinks a single hike would not be enough to meet the goal on time. The BoE raised Bank Rate to 4% from 3.75% last week, its first increase in more than three years.

NIESR Director David Aikman said the economy had held up better than expected this year but now faces the twin pressures of higher energy prices and rising global borrowing costs. He said the policy response on both the monetary and fiscal sides is a domestic choice, and that decisions on both fronts are being put off.

On the fiscal side, NIESR said finance minister John Healey’s October budget did little to strengthen the public finances. Difficult decisions on tax and spending had largely been deferred, it said, and plans to reduce borrowing lacked credibility.

Growth has been more resilient than expected. NIESR raised its 2026 forecast to 1.6% from 1.1%, citing stronger government spending and business investment, partly driven by AI. It expects growth to slow to 1.1% in 2027 and 0.9% in 2028. Unemployment is projected to edge up from 4.8% in 2026 to a peak of 5.1% in 2031, with wage growth averaging 3.9% over the next three years.

The gap between NIESR’s single-hike forecast and market pricing will be tested at the BoE’s next meetings, with energy prices and wage settlements the key data to watch.

NIESR is Britain’s longest-established independent economic research institute. It was founded in 1938 and is based in London. It is a non-profit charity, independent of government and political parties, and it publishes regular forecasts for the UK and global economies, which are widely watched alongside those from the Bank of England and the government’s fiscal watchdog. Its director, David Aikman, joined in 2023 from the Bank of England, where he worked on financial stability.

[2026-11-11 00:22 UTC] PBOC is expected to set the USD/CNY reference rate at 6.6855 – Reuters estimate
URL: https://investinglive.com/central-banks/pboc-is-expected-to-set-the-usd-cny-reference-rate-at-6-6855-reuters-estimate/
Tags: CNY, CNH, pboc
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summary

PBOC CNY reference rate setting for the trading session ahead.

The People’s Bank of China is due to set the daily USD/CNY reference rate at around 0115 GMT (2115 US Eastern time), a fixing that remains one of the most closely watched signals in Asian foreign exchange markets.

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system, under which the renminbi (yuan) is allowed to trade within a prescribed band around a central reference rate, or midpoint, set each trading day by the PBOC. The current trading band permits the currency to move plus or minus 2% from the official midpoint during onshore trading hours.

Each morning, the PBOC determines the midpoint based on a range of inputs. These include the previous day’s closing price, movements in major currencies, particularly the US dollar, broader international FX conditions, and domestic economic considerations such as capital flows, growth momentum and financial stability objectives. The midpoint is not a purely mechanical calculation, allowing policymakers discretion to guide market expectations.

Once the midpoint is announced, onshore USD/CNY is free to trade within the allowable band. If market pressures push the yuan toward either edge of that range, the central bank may step in to smooth volatility. Intervention can take the form of direct buying or selling of yuan, adjustments to liquidity conditions, or guidance through state-owned banks.

As a result, the daily fixing is often interpreted as a policy signal rather than just a technical reference point. A stronger-than-expected CNY midpoint is typically read as a sign the PBOC is leaning against depreciation pressure, while a weaker fixing for the CNY can indicate tolerance for a softer currency, often in response to dollar strength or domestic economic headwinds.

In periods of heightened global volatility, such as shifts in US rate expectations, trade tensions or capital flow pressures, the fixing takes on added significance. For investors, it provides insight into Beijing’s currency priorities, balancing competitiveness, capital stability and financial market confidence.

Summary

Japan’s October producer prices rose 7.1% y/y, above the 6.5% forecast and up from 6.8%, as oil, chemicals, steel and utilities climbed and a weaker yen lifted import costs. The beat further strengthens the case for a Bank of Japan hike in December.

The upside surprise adds to the case for a Bank of Japan hike in December. It should give the yen some support at the margin, although aFed that is also on a hiking path after a hot US CPIlimits how much the rate gap narrows. Oil is the key swing factor, because crude and fuel imports drove most of October’s import price jump. Any further rise in energy prices tied to the Iran war would feed directly into Japan’s pipeline inflation and strengthen the hawks’ argument. For Japanese government bonds, a firmer inflation backdrop keeps upward pressure on yields. For equities, rising input costs are a risk to margins for manufacturers that cannot pass them on.

Cheap rice is masking it, but energy, materials and a weak yen are pushing Japan’s pipeline inflation higher again, and that leaves little reason for the BoJ to wait.

Japan’s corporate goods price index rose 7.1% y/y in October, above the 6.5% forecast and up from 6.8% in September

Prices rose 0.7% m/m against a 0.3% forecast, after a revised 0.2% decline in September

Petroleum and coal, chemicals, iron and steel, and utilities drove the gain, while lower rice prices dragged agriculture down 6.6% on the month

Yen-based import prices jumped 3.3% m/m and are up nearly 25% y/y, led by crude oil and fuels

The yen weakened about 1% over the month, adding to import costs

The data strengthen expectations of a Bank of Japan hike in December, after a hold at 1.25% in October

Japan’s wholesale inflation picked up again in October, as producer prices rose faster than forecast and added weight to expectations that the Bank of Japan will raise interest rates in December. The corporate goods price index rose 7.1% from a year earlier, Bank of Japan data showed on Thursday. That was above the 6.5% median forecast and up from 6.8% in September. On the month, prices climbed 0.7%, more than double the 0.3% expected, after a revised 0.2% decline in September.

The rebound ends two months of slowing in the annual rate, and it was driven mainly by energy and industrial materials. On the month, petroleum and coal products rose about 3%, chemicals about 2%, iron and steel 2%, and electricity, gas and water around 2.4%. Together, those four groups added roughly 0.6 percentage point to the monthly increase.

The headline would have been even stronger without food. Agricultural products fell 6.6% on the month and are down a quarter from a year ago, as cheaper newly harvested rice continues to drag on the index. Without that drag, the underlying push from manufactured goods and energy was close to a 1% monthly rise. That suggests pipeline pressure is spreading, not fading.

Import costs remain the main engine. The yen-based import price index jumped 3.3% in October and is nearly 25% higher than a year ago. Most of the monthly gain came from crude oil and other fuels, with energy imports up about 46% on the year in yen terms, as prices stay elevated during the Iran war. A 1% slide in the yen over the month added to the pressure. Imported memory chips and other electronics also rose sharply, echoing the firmness in technology goods prices seen elsewhere.

For the Bank of Japan, the data support a move at its December meeting. The central bank held its policy rate at 1.25% in late October in an 8-1 vote, with board member Hajime Takata pushing for an immediate hike. It also said underlying inflation had reached levels broadly consistent with its 2% target. A Bloomberg survey published before that meeting showed 83% of economists expecting a December increase.

Producer prices are an upstream gauge, and the BoJ places more weight on consumer inflation and wages. But with a weak yen and high oil prices, these costs are likely to keep feeding through to households, which makes a pause harder to justify.

A hotter than expected US inflation report has put an unusual question in front of the Federal Reserve: whether its next hike should be a double-sized one. UBS thinks the answer is still no. But the bank says October’s data strengthen the case for at least two more quarter-point increases and raise the risk that they come faster.

Headline consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% forecast. Core prices, which exclude food and energy, rose 0.3% against expectations of 0.2%. Price gains were broad, covering food, housing, recreation, medical care, electricity, airfares and delivery services. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped 11 basis points to close to 5%, and the S&P 500 fell 0.4% on Tuesday.

Separately, Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said she favors at least two more quarter-point hikes and suggested the Fed could move more quickly this winter.

Fed funds futures now price about 28 basis points of tightening for December. That implies a full quarter-point hike and only a small chance of something larger.
